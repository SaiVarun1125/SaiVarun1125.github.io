§1Introduction

This artifact is the documentation for Interview Prep Coach, an AI chatbot I designed, built in Mizou and tested for the AIML-500 Machine Learning Fundamentals course (AI Lab 1.4). It runs mock data engineering interviews, asks one question at a time and gives a score with feedback. Artifact 1 shows how I research and explain a topic; this artifact shows how I build, test and improve an AI product. It is written for hiring managers and team leads in data and machine learning.

AudienceHiring managers and team leads in data and machine learning.

§3Description

The document covers practice with generative AI tools (the same prompt in three separate chats, a custom GPT exercise, and a research assistant called STORM), a problem-solving reflection, and the design of the chatbot using design thinking: Empathy, Define, Ideate, Prototype and Test. It lists the chatbot's instructions and ten rules, a test plan with 12 test cases, and the results of two rounds of live testing. It also covers Mizou's terms and privacy, the design impact of those terms, and the open questions I still have.

§4Objective

To show that I can turn a real need into a working AI tool and test it against clear criteria. The need: data engineers who want realistic interview practice and honest feedback but have no interviewer. The result: a coach that stays on topic, protects privacy, gives no guarantees or salary advice, and scores answers on content only. The first version showed a model answer too early, wrote the user's answer, listed a wrong gap and calculated a wrong average. After I rewrote the rules, all 12 tests passed.

Still openThe bot once skipped a question I asked for, and its rule 10 reminder did not appear. Both are logged in the document.

§5Process

  1. I defined the problem and the user: a data engineer preparing for interviews with limited evening time and no one to practice with.
  2. I wrote the chatbot's instructions and ten rules, and built it in Mizou using the Custom option, with a welcome message and a scoring guide.
  3. I wrote 12 test cases, one for each rule, and ran them in live sessions.
  4. I found the problems above in the first round and rewrote the rules to fix each one.
  5. I retested the failed and partial cases, and all passed. I recorded the two remaining problems in the document.

§7Value Proposition

Unique Value

I do not stop at a chatbot that looks right. I wrote a test for every rule, recorded where the bot failed, fixed the rules and retested. That process caught problems a quick demo would have missed.

Relevance

Teams that deploy AI need people who can define what a system must not do, test it with real use cases and report weaknesses honestly. That is the skill this artifact shows, and it fits my work in data engineering, where checking output matters as much as building it.

§8AI Use Statement
