§1Introduction

This artifact is a 10-slide timeline of artificial intelligence, from Alan Turing's 1950 paper to the launch of ChatGPT in 2022. I created it for the AIML-500 Machine Learning Fundamentals course. It is written for hiring managers and team leads in data and machine learning who want to see that I understand why AI advanced in waves and what made today's boom possible.

AudienceHiring managers and team leads in data and machine learning.

§3Description

The deck shows six eras of AI on one summary slide: the birth of AI (1943-1973), the first AI winter (1974-1980), the expert systems boom (1980-1986), the second AI winter (1987-1993), data and chess (1993-2011), and deep learning (2012 to now). Separate slides cover the milestones in each era, such as the Turing Test, the Dartmouth workshop, the perceptron, backpropagation, Deep Blue, Amazon's EC2, NVIDIA's CUDA, ImageNet, AlexNet, the Transformer and ChatGPT. Each AI winter has its own slide that lists its causes and its impacts. A closing slide compares the computing power used to train AlexNet in 2012 (470 petaFLOP) with PaLM in 2022 (about 2.5 billion), roughly 5 million times more, and lists the leading companies and models. The last slide gives seven sources and the AI disclosure.

§4Objective

To show that I can research a broad topic and explain it clearly to a non-technical reader. The deck argues one idea: the main obstacle to AI was not the ideas but the data and hardware. Backpropagation was popularized in 1986, but deep learning only took off after Amazon launched EC2 (2006), NVIDIA created CUDA (2007) and ImageNet was released (2009), and AlexNet combined them in 2012. Since about 2010, the computing power used to train AI models has doubled about every 6 months, compared with about every 20 months before (Roser, 2022).

§5Process

  1. I started with the three course resources (an IEEE article, a video history of AI, and an Our World in Data article) and added books and papers to support key claims.
  2. I used Claude to help gather and organize the milestones into six eras, then I reviewed and edited the results.
  3. I built a summary slide of the six eras, one slide for each period with its main milestones, and a separate slide for each AI winter showing causes and impacts.
  4. I added a slide on computing power and leading companies to explain why progress sped up after 2006.
  5. I posted the deck to the course discussion and asked classmates what should be added and whether the eras made sense, then exported it as a PDF for this portfolio.

Course resourcesBhat (2025), Putchuon (2024) and Roser (2022).

§7Value Proposition

Unique Value

I do not only list events. The deck argues that the limit on AI was data and computing power, and it backs that up with specific enablers (EC2, CUDA, ImageNet) and a measured jump in training computing. That link between data, computing and machine learning is close to my work as a data engineer.

Relevance

Hiring managers in data and machine learning need people who can explain why models succeed or fail in terms of data and computing resources, not only algorithms. The two AI winters show the cost of over-promising, and the deck shows I can explain that clearly to a mixed audience.
