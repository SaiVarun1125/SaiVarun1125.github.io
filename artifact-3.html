Machine Learning vs. Deep Learning: Choosing the Right Approach

§1Introduction

This artifact is a written comparison of machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL), created for the AIML-500 Machine Learning Fundamentals course (assignment 2.3). It uses two examples from the lesson, email spam detection for ML and autonomous driving for DL, and explains why each approach fits its problem and why the other does not. Artifact 1 shows research and Artifact 2 shows building and testing; this artifact shows technical reasoning. It is written for hiring managers and team leads in data and machine learning.

AudienceHiring managers and team leads in data and machine learning.

§3Description

The report has an introduction, two case studies, a comparison table, a conclusion, an AI disclosure and references. Each case study names a real-world application, gives three reasons the chosen approach is suitable, and gives three reasons the other approach is not.

Machine learning: email spam detection

Real-world example: Apache SpamAssassin. Suitable because spam can be spotted with features people define by hand, it runs fast on ordinary servers, and its decisions are easy to explain. Deep learning is not suitable because it adds cost, can overfit small or sparse data, and is hard to explain.

Deep learning: autonomous driving

Real-world example: Waymo's driving perception. Suitable because networks learn features from raw pixels, handle visual variation and improve with more data. Traditional ML is not suitable because hand-built features are fragile, raw pixels are too high-dimensional, and several tasks must run at once.

§4Objective

To show that I can choose the right modeling approach for a problem and justify the choice. The report argues that the choice depends on the structure of the data and the requirements of the system: structured data, strict time requirements and clear audit trails favor traditional machine learning, while complex unstructured data with endless real-world variation favors deep learning.

§5Process

  1. I selected one machine learning example and one deep learning example from the course lesson.
  2. I found a real-world application for each, Apache SpamAssassin and Waymo, and checked them against published sources.
  3. For each case, I wrote why the chosen approach is suitable and why the other is not, tied to the data, speed, cost and explainability of the problem.
  4. I built a comparison table covering data type, feature extraction, data requirements, compute needs and interpretability.
  5. I added citations, an AI disclosure and a reference list, then reviewed and edited the report.

§7Value Proposition

Unique Value

I do not treat deep learning as the default answer. The report explains when a simpler model is the better choice, for reasons of cost, speed and explainability, and when deep learning is worth its cost. That kind of judgment saves teams time and money.

Relevance

Hiring managers in data and machine learning need people who can match a method to a problem and explain the trade-offs to non-technical colleagues. This report shows I can do that, and it connects to my work as a data engineer, where choosing the right tool matters.
